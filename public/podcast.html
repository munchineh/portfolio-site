Turning flashcards into a podcast

A Korean listening podcast written from my Sogang syllabus, voiced with AI and played in a web app built for shadowing. Free, translated into ten languages, and shared with a link.

Product, UX, content and voice direction

Voicing runs on a free tier, about two 20-minute episodes a day.

01

Listening practice that sticks to the syllabus

Each Sogang book packs in hundreds of expressions, and flashcards only ever give me one sentence at a time.

I wanted long, natural conversations to train my ear and shadow along to, using only what we're learning in class. I tried NotebookLM first. It improvised its own chat, spoke at native speed and pulled in vocab well outside the books.

On syllabusOnly what's in the books

Every expression, grammar point and word comes from the Sogang Level 3 and 4 handbooks.

Sounds realLike a drama, at a pace I can follow

Real situations and speech levels, read slowly enough to repeat after.

ShareableFor my classmates too

Subtitles in their languages, nothing to install and no account. Just a link.

02

Before the podcast, I gave 4,000+ flashcards a voice with ElevenLabs. Getting them to sound like people took a lot of tuning.

Then the maths caught up with me. One level of podcast scripts came to about 330,000 characters, close to three months of ElevenLabs credits. So the long-form audio moved to Gemini's free tier, and everything I'd learned about voices came along with it.

03

Textbook lines, moved into real life

Textbook dialogues are tidy. One speaker at a time, in a quiet room, every word said clearly. Real conversations aren't.

Each card gets a situation where people would actually say it: a planner reporting delays to her team leader, a job interview, a confession over soju. Speech levels follow the relationship, so you hear 합쇼체 to a director and 반말 between friends. The words stay exactly as the book teaches them.

OverlapsPeople talk over each other

Interruptions cut in a split second early, the way they do at work and over dinner.

NoiseNot every room is quiet

Restaurant chatter and other background sound under the scenes.

FeelingExcited, tired, mumbling

Lines carry real emotion, laughs and trailing-off words, so your ear gets used to how people really sound.

04

The right model for each job

No single AI did everything well, so each job went to the tool that does it best, or for the least credit.

ElevenLabs is the better Korean voice, but it charges by the character. Gemini 3.8 is free and reads a whole scene in one go, but it comes with limits I had to design around.

ElevenLabs v4Gemini 3.8 TTS
CostPer character. One level of scripts was about three months of creditsFree tier, 10 requests a day
DialogueOne line at a timeA whole scene per request, so replies flow naturally
Korean detailVery reliableGood, with the odd slip in long takes
VoicesA large library, any number per clipTwo per request, and voices can drift

Each of Gemini's limits got its own workaround: side characters are voiced separately and spliced in, every line carries a "same voice as before" cue, a voice check catches drift, and runs are scheduled just after the free quota resets.

05

Korean is written one way and said another

Sounds change when they meet the sound next to them. AI voices sometimes read the spelling instead.

Every language does this. In English, "handbag" comes out closer to "hambag". Korean has clear rules for it, and learners pick them up by ear, so a voice that reads the letters teaches the wrong sound.

Nasalisation
국물said as궁물

A hard ㄱ softens into a nasal ㅇ before ㅁ. "Soup broth".

Palatalisation
같이said as가치

ㅌ before 이 turns into ㅊ, like "got you" becoming "gotcha".

Tensing
똑바로said as똑빠로

ㅂ hardens into ㅃ after ㄱ. "Straight" or "properly".

  1. Respell what the voice reads

    A set of rules rewrites each sentence the way it's said before it goes to the voice. Learners still see the normal spelling. This fixed most of the misreadings on the Anki cards.

  2. Find out what the checks can't hear

    Speech-to-text writes down spelling, so a wrong sound still comes back as the right word. Pronunciation had to be caught before voicing, and by ear.

  3. Test the same sentence three ways

    One sentence with six tricky spots, read as written, with light fixes, and fully respelled, by both ElevenLabs and Gemini. All three sounded almost the same, so the voices mostly know the rules already.

  4. Fix the few slips that are left

    They happened across the gap between two words in long takes, like 고객 문의 in a pointed question. Now those phrases are joined up, numbers are written out in Hangul, and a daily Claude review marks the risky spots before each episode is voiced.

One pronunciation tool shared by the cards and the podcast, with no old audio remade.
06

A player built for shadowing

Follow along syllable by syllable, then replay the line and say it back.

Grammar is filled purple and vocab is dotted. Tap either to open the card behind it, with notes and every other place it shows up in the episode.

Replay line

One tap repeats the current line, so shadowing never means scrubbing.

Speed control

0.7× to 1.2×, for first listens and then real pace.

Picks up where you left off

Each episode remembers its place, no sign-in needed.

07

Localised for a classroom of ten languages

My classmates come from all over, so the subtitles and the interface switch together.

Pick a language once and every label, title and subtitle follows. Traditional Chinese is generated from Simplified, with Taiwanese wording. The whole thing installs to the home screen, and sharing it is just sending the link.
