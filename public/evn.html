Helping shoppers find their fit

I run EVN Archive, a Shopify store that sells independent Korean menswear overseas. Many of its pieces come in one size only, known in Korea as free size, and they weren't selling. This case covers how I found out why, and what I changed.

Role
Designer and founder. UX, UI, content and front-end
Timeline
May 2026 to now
Tools
Shopify (Liquid, CSS, JavaScript), Claude Design
Team
Just me
What came of itSept 2026
4free-size sales in the sizing tool's first week, after none in the two months before

The sizing tool, a 20% free-size offer and social posts about each piece all went out that week, so the result belongs to all three.

01

Finding out why free size wasn't selling

Some of my favourite pieces in the archive were also the hardest to sell.

Three free-size jackets drew hundreds of visits and almost no add-to-carts. Shoppers outside Korea are used to choosing from several sizes. Most had never seen free size, so they couldn't tell how a piece would sit on them.

02

Building a sizing tool from each garment's measurements

A size chart doesn't help when there's only one size.

Shoppers enter their height and weight. The tool compares them with the garment's own measurements and answers in plain words: snug, recommended or relaxed. It reads the Size & Fit copy already on each product, so a new piece needs no extra set-up.

03

Carrying one answer through the whole shop

Nobody wants to enter their measurements on every product page.

Shoppers enter their details once, on the homepage or the Find your fit page. After that, every product tile and product page shows how that piece will sit on them, and the shop can filter down to their best fits.

04

What got in the way

The hardest part was the sizing logic.

I measured every piece, tried each one on myself, and worked out the rules through trial and error with an AI model: it suggested a formula, and I checked it against how the piece actually fit. The tool wasn't the only change either. It launched in the same week as the 20% offer and the social posts, and I think it took all three to move the number.

What I took from itEffective design is often several touchpoints working together.
